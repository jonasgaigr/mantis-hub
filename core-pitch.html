A hub for sensor-based biodiversity monitoring

MANTIS will form the connective tissue for automated monitoring with novel technologies based on image and sound across Europe.

  • Connect data acquisition – align protocols, deployments and annotations, and extend spatial and taxonomic coverage. Package 1
  • Connect the data – harmonise datasets and metadata into a quality-controlled flow from images and sounds to indicators, including transnational benchmarking of AI models. Package 2
  • Connect the community – link the users of these technologies to one another, and to the skills and resources that sustain a transnational effort. Package 3

Aarhus University with the Nature Conservation Agency CZ · 1 January 2027 – 30 September 2028 · Core Activity 3 primary, Core Activities 2 and 4 secondary · a progenitor for a Thematic Hub associated with an EBOCC.

45 seconds. Say what kind of thing MANTIS is before saying what it does: infrastructure for monitoring itself, not another technology and not another taxon’s dataset. “Connective tissue” is the proposal’s own phrase and the one image worth repeating; the three bullets are the three packages, and they return on the figure two slides from now.

Building a system for the European sensors

The devices are in the ground already – AMI traps and acoustic recorders across 11 countries in the ABMS pilot (2.6.5), an AMI trap and a CamAlien unit in 10 countries in the IAS pilot (2.6.2), acoustic recorders in ten countries in BiodivPond (2.6.8), four countries in TABMON, and insect camera traps in over 30 countries through MAMBO and related projects. They are operated as independent national efforts, and three bottlenecks follow – not one of them national.

  1. Verification cannot keep up. Image and sound data has grown faster than the community’s capacity to verify it, calibrate the AI models processing it, and make the outputs comparable across countries and taxa.
  2. AI accuracy is unknown outside its training context. Moth classifiers trained on Danish and UK expert-verified images performed poorly in Slovakia, Croatia, Bulgaria and Israel.
  3. Sensors still need field visits. A substantial share of deployed acoustic recorders require manual retrieval rather than remote transfer, limiting temporal coverage and cost-efficiency.

Left unaddressed, these problems risk automated monitoring becoming a source of fragmented, incomparable, or even unprocessed national data – repeating, at greater cost and complexity, the exact coordination problem transnational monitoring should be designed to solve.

90 seconds, and this is the slide that has to land. First half: nobody is being asked to believe in an unproven technology, and Biodiversa+ has already paid for a good share of these deployments. Second half: model calibration, sensor connectivity and annotation protocol design are not country-specific technical problems, so they should not be solved redundantly across up to 27 Member States. Read the quote out – it is the proposal’s own sentence.

MANTIS is one quality-controlled chain from a sensor in the field to a policy indicator, and the three packages each own a different stretch of it.

90 seconds, and this is the deck. Walk the chain left to right once, then stop on the numbered gates: deployment registration, expert verification, AI benchmarking, standards conformance. The gates are the activity. Almost everything either side of them already exists somewhere in Europe, which is why this is a hub rather than a new monitoring scheme.

Sustaining the System

…national and regional automated monitoring efforts using image and sound technologies will function as a connected, transnational system rather than isolated national deployments.

  • One place every deployment is registered – 100% of active AMI, CamAlien and acoustic deployments across ≥8 partner countries, machine-readable and Camtrap DP-compliant. O1 · 30 Sep 2028
  • Sensor data reaching policy without bespoke analysis – ≥4 indicators live on the MANTIS dashboard, each mapped to NRR Art. 10 / EU-PoMS, the Habitats or Birds Directive or IAS Regulation 1143/2014, auto-updating from GBIF. O2 · 30 Sep 2028
  • Data to benchmark AI against – ≥5,000 expert-verified annotations published under FAIR principles, ≥20% reviewed by experts from ≥2 countries. O3 · 31 Dec 2027
  • Partners who can run this themselves – 25 summer-school participants from ≥12 countries. O4 · 30 Jun 2028
  • A pathway beyond Biodiversa+ – a roadmap endorsed by ≥2/3 of partners, naming ≥2 concrete funding or hosting pathways. O5 · 30 Jun 2028

€796,850.18 requested – CORE + ENHANCED for the two leads (€422,535.30), plus ≈€54,494.36 for each of six additional participating partners. The activity descopes cleanly: fewer partners means proportionally fewer scaled deployments, not a thinner version of everything. Nothing here needs continued funding to survive 2028 – GitHub Pages and GBIF carry the outputs, and statutory Habitats and Birds Directive duties carry the verification capacity.

90 seconds, and it serves two rooms at once. Every objective carries a date and a threshold, so at the end of the activity you can tell whether it was met without holding a discussion about it: O3 is the one everything else depends on, O5 the one that decides whether this outlives the money. For a partner weighing whether to join, the commitment behind the per-partner figure is four person-months, two in-person attendances, and equipment that stays with you. If asked about maintenance: no new secretariat and no hosting bill – NCA CZ holds the reference datasets under its reporting duties, Aarhus underwrites media storage to 2031, and the registration system and benchmarking transfer to BiodivPond, ABMS and the IAS pilot.
